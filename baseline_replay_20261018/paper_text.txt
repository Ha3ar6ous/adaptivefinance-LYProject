Abstract—Income for gig and platform-based workers can vary
significantly on a day-to-day basis and, therefore, makes tradi-
tional ratio-based approaches to financial planning challenging to
apply. In this paper, we introduce a risk-aware financial decision-
making framework that evaluates the readiness of a gig worker
to invest in any investment product. The framework consists
of six modules: income forecasting module with a lightweight
mathematical solution; income-volatility classifier based on the
coefficient of variation; multi-factor financial health scoring
model; deterministic rule-based decision router that restricts ac-
cess to investment features in unsafe scenarios; micro-investment
recommendation engine; and a large language model (LLM)
explanation layer, which gives natural-language explanations for
every recommendation. In contrast to the systems where an
investment recommendation is the main goal, our framework
focuses on financial safety: users with low emergency fund
amount, excessive debt, and high income-volatility are advised
to take stabilization actions first and not to make investments.
We evaluate the framework by performing empirical studies
using synthetic gig-workers’ income datasets. Results show that
the mathematical forecasting technique produces results with
the same level of accuracy, but not better than the historical
mean baseline (MAE: 352.07 vs. 348.48) and that the rule-based
decision router successfully gates all unsafe scenarios.
Index Terms—gig economy, income volatility, financial health
score, risk-aware decision routing, micro-investment, explainable
AI, financial inclusion
I. INTRODUCTION
There have been significant developments in the platform-
based gig economy in India in recent years, with Economic
Survey 2025-26 projecting around 12 million gig and platform
workers in FY2024-25, compared to 7.7 million in FY2020-
21 [16]. The workers employed by delivery, ride-hailing, and
freelance platforms like Zomato, Swiggy, Blinkit, and Uber
usually generate income fluctuating daily based on demand,
incentives, and hours worked. Earlier studies reported income
insecurity and lack of social security coverage among workers
in the digital labour platforms [8]. The situation may expose
the workers to income shocks and hinder financial planning.
In this case, standard financial advisory practices, which are
based on assumption of fixed monthly income, would not be
adequate for such workers.
Conventional personal finance applications and robo-
advisors usually concern themselves only about recommending
a suitable portfolio for the user after determining that he wants
to invest in something, without checking whether he should
be investing or not at this point of his life. It is indeed an
important issue for gig workers because premature investment
would worsen their financial condition.
In this paper, we present a pipeline that attempts to address
this problem by treating investment-readiness as a primary
decision that is made prior to the recommendation of any
investments. This pipeline consists of four components: the
first step entails using the income data to make a short-term
prediction and volatility classification, which in combination
with the profile data (balance, debt, expenses, savings) of
the user, goes into making a multi-factorial score on their
financial health; the second component involves using a deter-
ministic decision router to route the user either to building
an emergency fund, decreasing their debt level, or making
conservative investments depending on the score and a set
of safety rules; the final two components involve passing only
the users qualified for making investments to a micro-investing
engine followed by an explanation layer.
It is critical to be precise about what is, and is not, claimed
about the underlying technologies. The income forecasting
and volatility classifiers are implemented as mathematical and
statistical descriptive approaches – rolling means, constrained
slope, and coefficient of variation, rather than predictive al-
gorithms like ARIMA, Random Forest, or XGBoost. The
financial well-being metric and decision router are purely
deterministic and rule-based elements. The only element in
the present system that has been learned or generated is the
LLM explanation module, and its role has been carefully
confined to generating language from the results computed by
other modules without carrying out any financial calculation
or gating itself. This paper describes the present state of the
implementation and evaluation of the system, and explicitly
identifies which elements of the original idea (ARIMA, XG-
Boost, Random Forest, Gemini API) were not realized in this
version of the system.
The contributions of this paper are:
•A safety-first decision pipeline that gates micro-
investment recommendations behind an explicit financial
health and emergency-fund check, rather than presenting
investment options unconditionally.
•A lightweight, dependency-free mathematical income
forecasting method and a coefficient-of-variation-based
volatility classifier suited to short, sparse income histories
typical of new gig-worker users.
•A multi-factor financial health scoring formulation that
combines liquidity, debt safety, income stability, forecast
trend, and data consistency into a single interpretable
score.
•An LLM-based explanation layer that is architecturally
sandboxed from the numerical decision logic, together
with a deterministic fallback mechanism for cases where
the LLM call fails.
•An honest empirical evaluation of each component using
synthetic gig-worker datasets, including a comparison of
the forecasting method against a naive baseline and a
threshold-consistency evaluation of the volatility classi-
fier.
II. RELATEDWORK
A. Income Instability and Financial Uncertainty of Gig Work-
ers
Existing research demonstrates that income instability is
greater among gig workers than it is in the context of regular
salary, and this instability and lack of control over income
affect the process of financial planning [15]. Sun [1] provides
the mathematical and statistical study of the income of gig
workers in the platform economy and concludes that gig
income is unstable and provides an understanding of features
contributing to the instability. Such works have established
the importance of income volatility as one of the primary
issues in the process of financial planning for gig workers,
although being descriptive and offline, in the sense that they
do not provide the continuous process of income tracking and
prediction.
B. Measure of Financial Health
Conventional techniques used to measure financial health,
which are based on the use of Z-score models validated
for corporate financial health measurement [2], are based
on accounting ratios that can be determined using periodic
stable financial statements. Static ratio-based measurement
techniques cannot be applied in case of irregular income flows
of the individual in question, since the assumptions of fixed
reporting period are violated. The proposed model calculates
a weighted dynamic financial health score by considering fac-
tors like liquidity, debt, income stability, forecast trends, and
consistency of the provided financial information measured
relatively to the user’s recent income data.
C. ML-Based Scoring and Credit Inclusion
In her survey paper, Hashi [3] studies the use of ML-based
fintech solutions for credit scoring and financial inclusion
in the context of the gig economy, noting that the models
that use combinations of various financial and behavior-based
inputs are superior in terms of risk assessment to those based
on a single accounting ratio. However, this body of research
focuses on the problems of credit scoring and risk prediction
of loans and does not consider a broader problem of measuring
financial health and readiness for investments.
D. Automated Financial Guidance and Robo-Advisory
Bhagavanji and Nagarathinam [4] discuss an AI-enabled
financial planner dashboard that gives trend insights to the
user. In addition, the authors give empirical data on robo-
advising of small-scale investors in the setting of employees’
savings programs, showing that automated systems are capable
of creating reasonable portfolios at low costs. The limitation
typical for these studies is that the recommendation process
is considered unconditional, that is, upon receiving a request
from the user, the system creates a recommendation regardless
of the fact whether his/her financial standing allows the user
to invest. The suggested framework differs from the described
approach by introducing a deterministic decision router be-
tween the assessment of the user’s financial standing and the
creation of a recommendation, redirecting people who fall
below the safety threshold to emergency fund accumulation
and debt repayment rather than giving them recommendations
for investments.
E. Explainability in AI-Assisted Financial Systems
The importance of the explainability of AI-enabled financial
systems targeted at users without specialist background has
been emphasized [12]. As for the framework suggested in
this paper, its explainability does not come from applying an
opaque learned model as a scoring or gating function, but from
making the numerical logic deterministic and employing large
language models only as an interface rendering the already
calculated numbers. This approach sacrifices certain flexibility
of end-to-end recommendation learning systems for greater
auditability.
F . Positioning of the Present Work
The above literature review covers a variety of topics such as
income volatility, financial health assessment, ML-based credit
scoring, automated advisory dashboard creation, and robo-
advisory investment suggestions as independent subjects and
not necessarily in a way that conditions the recommendations
with an explicit safety verification of the user. The innovation
of this research is neither an introduction of a novel algorithm
for any of these tasks but the incorporation of continuous in-
come assessment, health scoring, deterministic safety filtering,
constrained micro-investment, and LLM-based explanations
into one pipeline that targets irregular daily income users.
III. PROBLEMSTATEMENT ANDRESEARCHOBJECTIVES
A. Problem Statement
Gig economy users experience irregular income flows,
insufficient savings, and restricted access to traditional fi-
nancial advice and planning. Current financial and robo-
advisors assume the user’s financial readiness for investments
Fig. 1. System architecture of the proposed risk-aware financial decision
framework.
and concentrate only on the portfolio construction without
checking if the user is financially ready for that. As a result,
there is a possibility of vulnerable users being recommended
investment products even before they are financially stable and
safe enough for investing.
B. Research Objectives
The framework aims at solving the following problems:
1) To continuously feed the income data of a gig worker
on a daily basis, analyze it and come up with a short
term income forecast and volatility category.
2) To calculate a single number score (0-100) that re-
flects the financial health by combining indicators of
income stability, liquidity, indebtedness, income growth
tendency and data consistency.
3) To make a deterministic decision based on safety rules if
a user should save money in an emergency fund, pay off
some debt or he/she is qualified for conservative micro-
investments.
4) To offer a set of micro-investment opportunities limited
by the user’s eligibility criteria (affordability, risk toler-
ance, volatility safety) and sorted in descending order of
recommendation importance.
5) To explain the recommendation result in natural lan-
guage without changing the numerical output.
6) To test every element of the framework empirically on
a synthetic gig-workers dataset and provide unbiased
results, both for the positive and negative cases.

B. Design Principle: Safety Before Recommendation
The primary architectural design decision made here is the
placement of the decision router after the financial health score
but before the investment recommendation, not the other way
around. Investment suggestions will only be calculated and
prioritized when the decision router is allowed to take the
conservative_investingaction; otherwise, the output
will include only blockers and stabilization actions (e.g.,
”Build emergency fund,” ”Reduce debt”). Such an architecture
aims to ensure that the system will never offer any investment
products to someone who does not have enough safety margins
(in terms of having an emergency fund and debt level), no
matter how good other indicators may look.
C. Data Sources
Due to the lack of a large scale labeled gig-income dataset in
the real world, income, volatility, and investment-option data
were derived using a combination of publicly available Kaggle
data (data on e-commerce/gig delivery analysis, freelancer
earnings, delivery partner income prediction, Indian personal
finance and spending behavior, and mutual funds data) and
existing benchmarking numbers. More specifically, an earn-
ings benchmark number of around 102 per hour for Zomato
delivery partners in 2025 was taken into consideration while
creating the income-generating assumptions for the generated
synthetic datasets [17]. 102 is the average earnings per hour

F . LLM-Based Explanation Layer
The explanation layer uses the Groq API with the
llama-3.1-8b-instantmodel, decoding temperature
0.2, and request timeout 12,000 ms. The backend creates
a JSON snapshot with the health score, the weakest
contributors, router actions and blockers, forecast summary
statistics, volatility label, and the balance, spending,
debt, and risk appetite of the user, and provides this
snapshot to the LLM with the explicit instruction to use
only the given values and not to derive any new figures
or invent any investment instruments. The LLM must
provide a strictly defined JSON object with nine fields:
overview,healthInsight,forecastInsight,
decisionInsight,investmentInsight,reasons,
actionPlan,watchOut, andtone. In case of
API failure, timeout, or output not conforming to the
JSON schema, the backend uses a deterministic template
(fallbackFromSnapshot) to create equivalent natural-
language text based on the snapshot, without involving any
API. By design, there is no way for the LLM to modify
the health score, router decision, or investment allocation; it
works strictly downstream of these deterministic procedures.
VI. EXPERIMENTALSETUP
A. Datasets
Two types of datasets have been used for evaluation pur-
poses. Firstly, income datasets with 16 files and 1,980 total
entries spanning 10 gig economy platforms such as Uber,
Swiggy, Blinkit, and Zomato (capitalization varies in the file-
names) (data/normal/anddata/volatility/) were

the complete health-score/router/investment computation was
measured in the production environment directly.

VIII. LIMITATIONS
Several important limitations of this project should be
mentioned. First, the income forecasting module uses a math-
ematical trend-projection model and does not provide better
predictions than the naive baseline of historical mean in
the evaluation dataset; this module should not be considered
to provide any proven predictive ability. Second, a 35.6%
agreement between the volatility classifier predictions and
the ground-truth labels in the evaluation datasets suggest that
there might be some misalignment between the calibration
window/thresholds used in this classifier and the process
of creating those ground-truth labels, and this problem has
not been addressed yet. Third, the financial health score
and decision router components, while consistent in their
predictions in synthetic scenarios, have not been evaluated
against real user outcomes. Fourth, the real-world latency,
failure rate, and explanations quality of the LLM explanation
layer when used via the live API have not been assessed in
this evaluation. Fifth, there were no automatic unit testing
systems (such as Jest or PyTest) implemented for this codebase
at the moment of the evaluation, limiting the assurance of
regression safety of this software. Sixth, the investment rec-
ommendations generated by the system were not backtested
against real market performance, and no claims are made
about the profitability or other financial outcomes of following
these recommendations. Finally, the synthetic datasets used
both during development and evaluation, while based on the
published baseline statistics, do not represent all behavioral
and platform-specific complexity of the gig worker income
streams.
IX. CONCLUSION ANDFUTUREWORK
In this paper, we introduced a risk-aware financial decision
pipeline for gig workers, which encompasses mathematical
income prediction, statistical volatility classification, multi-
dimensional financial health scoring, a deterministic safety-
gate decision router, a constrained micro-investment recom-
mendation engine, and an LLM-explanation module, which
itself is constrained to natural language representations of
deterministic predictions. Empirically testing the system on
synthetic gig worker data demonstrates that our income fore-
casting model is no more accurate than a trivial baseline;
that the classification results of our volatility classifier only
show limited agreement with the ground truth labels, possibly
because of threshold or window mismatches; that our financial
health score is behaving consistent in synthetic settings; and
that our decision router always rejects investment recommen-
dation for unsafe scenarios.
Future research directions include: using a calibration pro-
cess for volatility classification that is tied to the rolling
window directly and which is also consistent with the labeling
methodology; testing the forecasting module on more base-
lines and for longer holdout times; performing a formal us-
ability and comprehension study of the generated explanations
by the LLM on gig workers; developing an automated testing
suite for the health-score, router, and investment-engine logic;
and, in case of access to a sufficiently sized real-world income
data set, using trained models in place of the mathematical
forecasting and volatility classification methods, tested using
the same honest baseline comparison framework of this paper.
